Four runs and two gym sessions per week. Saturday is a full rest day so Sunday's long run lands on fresh legs.
Read this before week 1. You have averaged 10 km a week for four weeks and then covered 20 km across one weekend. That is a doubling of load in a single step, and with a knee history it is the classic pattern that precedes an overuse injury. Week 1 below is deliberately close to what you have just done rather than a jump beyond it. If the knee is sore or swollen on any morning, replace that day's run with the gym session or full rest. Losing one session costs nothing; losing three weeks costs the race.

How to get the files off your Forerunner 235

Option A — straight from the watch (richest data). Plug the 235 into your computer with the charging clip. It mounts as a drive. Open GARMIN/ACTIVITY/ and copy the .FIT files across. These are the raw files and contain every second of heart rate from your strap.

Option B — from Garmin Connect on the web. Open the activity, click the gear icon at the top right, then Export to TCX or Export Original (which gives you the .FIT). TCX carries heart rate and pace and is the easier of the two to work with.

Option C — bulk export everything. Garmin Connect → Account settings → Data Management → Export Your Data. Garmin emails you a zip of your whole history within a day or two. Unzip it and drop the folder of .FIT files in above, all at once.

Training load — acute vs chronic

7-day load 28-day average
Load is distance weighted by intensity. Keeping the ratio of the two lines between 0.8 and 1.3 is the widely used heuristic for building fitness without spiking injury risk. Above 1.5 for more than a few days is where problems tend to appear, and with your knee that band matters more than it would otherwise.

Heart rate zones

Calculated from your lactate threshold heart rate, which is more reliable than the max-HR method and far more reliable than the 220-minus-age formula. Set your values in Settings.
How to find your threshold heart rate. After a good warm-up, run 30 minutes as hard as you can hold evenly on flat ground. Take your average heart rate over the final 20 minutes. That number is your LTHR. Do this in week 2 or 3 and again around week 9, because it moves as you get fitter. The 235 will not calculate it for you, but the strap gives you the data you need to read it off afterwards.

Equivalent performances at your current fitness

Riegel extrapolation with an exponent of 1.06. Useful as a sanity check: if you cannot run the 10 km time shown, the half marathon target is not yet realistic and the honest move is to adjust the goal rather than the effort.

Weigh yourself the same way every time: same morning of the week, straight after waking, after the toilet, before eating or drinking, without clothes. Day-to-day swings of a kilogram are almost entirely water and gut contents, which is why only the four-week average line is worth reading.

Morning resting heart rate

—
Optional, but it is the single most useful number you can collect with the strap outside of running. Lie still for two minutes after waking and take the lowest steady figure. A rise of seven beats or more above your normal, held for two or three mornings, usually means fatigue, a coming illness, dehydration or alcohol, and it almost always shows up before your legs tell you anything.

Add a reading

Once a week is exactly right. Daily weighing adds noise, not information, and invites you to react to numbers that mean nothing.

On automatic Garmin sync

Direct sync with Garmin Connect needs credentials from the Garmin Connect Developer Program, which Garmin grants to registered companies rather than to individual athletes. There is no supported route for a personal app to pull your data automatically.

Unofficial libraries exist that log in as you and scrape the data. They work until Garmin changes something, and they sit outside Garmin's terms of service. Worth knowing about; not what this app does.

File import gives you identical data for the cost of about twenty seconds after each run, and it keeps your Garmin password out of it entirely.
